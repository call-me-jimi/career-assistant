Task: Simulate the first screening of this application, deciding whether to invite the candidate to a first interview.

Step 1 — Read the room before you judge. From the inputs, work out who reads this letter and what they value:
- the level of the role (individual contributor, lead, manager, executive) and how hands-on it is
- the company's type, stage, and culture (startup, scale-up, corporate, public sector, agency, ...) as far as the inputs show it
- the conventions of the letter's language and market (formality, salutation, length, how directly people sell themselves)
- the channel: a letter sent through a recruiter is read by the agency first, often for an anonymous client, so it cannot name company specifics the job ad doesn't give; judge it on whether the recruiter can confidently put the candidate forward
Hold the letter to that reader's standard, not a generic executive one.

Step 2 — Evaluate the cover letter against the CV, job description, and company context:
1. First impression (does the right level and scope come across in a 10-second skim?)
2. Relevance to the role (alignment with the responsibilities and requirements that matter most here)
3. Strength of evidence (credible achievements, business impact, placed early)
4. Tone and seniority fit (does the voice and scope match this role, this company, and this market?)
5. Personalization (genuine company or role context, as far as the channel allows; a clear reason for this move)
6. Weaknesses (unclear points, generic or AI-sounding phrasing, missing information) — only those that would change the decision

Rules for the lists:
- Weaknesses: only material ones. An empty list is a valid answer for a letter that is ready to send.
- Suggestions: each must be achievable from evidence in the CV and must not make the letter materially longer — prefer replacing something to adding something. If a gap can only be closed by experience the CV doesn't show, say so in "reasoning" instead of suggesting the letter claim it.

Return ONLY a single JSON object matching this schema, with the fields in this order — no prose, no code fences, no commentary:

  "suggestions": ["<concrete, actionable improvement, or leave the list empty>"],
  "reasoning": "<short paragraph explaining the score and decision, separating letter quality from candidate fit>",
  "overall_score": <float, 0.0-10.0>,
  "decision": "<YES | MAYBE | NO>"
}

Scoring guidance — how this reader would act on the letter:
- 9.0-10.0: Clear interview. The letter makes the case as well as this CV allows; edits would be polish.
- 7.5-8.9: Likely interview. One or two material fixes would strengthen it.
- 6.0-7.4: On the fence. The letter undersells or mis-pitches the candidate for this reader.
- < 6.0: Would not progress. Wrong pitch for the role, generic, or the fit gap is too wide.
A strong letter for a candidate with a real fit gap can still be a MAYBE; say which of the two drives the decision.
