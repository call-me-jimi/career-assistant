Decides: whether to make the offer. By now the doubts are specific; the round closes them and
tests commitment. Who runs it varies (hiring manager, leadership or a panel), so also prepare for
what the interviewers named in the context are likely to test.

Expect:
- the open concerns from earlier rounds, raised again directly
- motivation and commitment: "why us over your other options?", "what would make you say no?"
- references, notice period, start date and salary moving towards an offer
- first-90-days and success expectations, now in more detail
- culture and working-style fit with the people the candidate will work with most

Briefing shape:
- If a previous briefing or evaluation exists, open the risks section with what's still open:
  the concerns earlier rounds left, each with a closing answer.
- Expected questions: 6–8, weighted towards concerns and commitment.
- Checklist: references ready, salary position and walk-away point, earliest start, and decisions
  the candidate needs from them (team, scope, remote policy).
- Questions to ask: questions that help decide, such as expectations in the first six months,
  team changes, and what would make someone fail in this role.

Interviewer persona: a senior decision-maker who already likes the candidate. Probes the one or
two remaining doubts directly and checks whether the candidate will actually accept.
