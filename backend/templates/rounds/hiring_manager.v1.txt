Decides: whether this is the person the manager wants to own the problem. That means judgement,
ownership and real impact, tested through a few stories followed all the way down. Fewer
questions than a screen, and much longer follow-ups.

Expect:
- a short intro, then straight into one or two of the candidate's headline achievements: "what did
  *you* do", "how was it measured", "what was the baseline", "how does it connect to the business"
- judgement questions: a project you stopped or redirected, a decision against your team, pushback
  you had to navigate, how you decide ownership
- people questions: developing someone, raising the bar, handling underperformance, what you look
  for when hiring
- "do it here" questions: your first 90 days, how you would approach our problem X, what success
  looks like after 6–12 months
- the interviewer asking the candidate to assess *their* situation ("how mature do you think we
  are?")
- what you would want from me as your manager, and what you want to grow into

Hiring managers redirect when an answer drifts ("I meant more from the business side").
Answering a neighbouring question is the most common failure in this round.

Briefing shape:
- Expected questions: 6–8, with a likely follow-up chain for the top two ("they will then
  ask…").
- Stories: 3–5, each prepared at four depths: result, personal contribution, how it was measured
  against what, and business consequence or what you'd do differently.
- Add a short first-90-days view inside the positioning section: what you'd learn, fix and
  ship, grounded in the job ad and company description.
- Risks: the hiring concerns a manager would hold privately, with the evidence that answers each.
- Questions to ask: the team's current state, the biggest gap, how success is measured, what the
  manager needs most in the first six months.
- Leave salary and logistics out unless the context says the manager will raise them.

Interviewer persona: the manager who owns the role. Picks one claim from the CV and digs until
the candidate's own contribution and its business effect are clear. Redirects vague answers and
asks "why" and "what would you do here".
