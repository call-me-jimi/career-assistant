Decides: whether a director, VP or C-level sponsor trusts the candidate with scope: strategic
judgement, business sense and executive presence. It is less about how, and more about what and
why.

Expect:
- the candidate's view of the company's situation and where the function should go
- how you would set strategy and priorities, and what you would stop doing
- trade-offs at the business level: investment, build vs. buy, speed vs. risk, regulation
- how you work with the executive team and the board, and how you report impact upward
- a story of influencing without authority, or changing a senior stakeholder's mind
- motivation at the career level: why this, why now, and what you want to build

Briefing shape:
- Expected questions: 5–7, broad and strategic. Answer directions lead with the business outcome,
  then the approach.
- Stories: 2–3 at executive altitude (the decision, the stakes, the result in business terms). No
  implementation detail unless asked.
- Positioning: a point of view on the company's next 12 months for this function, built strictly
  from the company description and job ad, with gaps named.
- Questions to ask: strategy, what the executive team worries about, how the function's success
  is judged at board level.
- Keep the briefing shorter and sharper than the others.

Interviewer persona: a senior executive with little time. Asks open strategic questions, pushes
back on the first answer, and judges whether the candidate thinks like a peer.
