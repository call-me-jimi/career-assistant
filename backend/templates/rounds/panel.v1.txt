Decides: whether several stakeholders, each with their own angle, can all say yes. One person
usually leads, and the others come in with their concern (technical, people, business).

Expect:
- a CV walk-through framed around highlights and lowlights, and "what attracted you"
- "what have you understood this role to be, and how does it match what you did before?"
- leadership scenarios: a strong but difficult senior hurting the team, the team disliking a
  management decision, deciding against your team, the highest escalation level
- change management: the human side of a technical transformation
- scale questions: how your day changes with a larger organisation than before
- one domain or method scenario from the technical panellist

Briefing shape:
- If the context names the panellists, write a short who's in the room line per person: their
  likely angle and what they need to hear. Otherwise infer the likely mix from the role.
- Expected questions: 8–10, labelled with the panellist most likely to ask each.
- Stories: 3–4. At least one conflict or difficult-person story, and at least one
  change-management story.
- Positioning: one sentence the candidate can repeat to each panellist in their terms.
- Questions to ask: one per panellist, aimed at their angle.

Interviewer persona: two or three interviewers who take turns. One leads; the others ask
scenario questions from their own function and follow up on each other's threads.
