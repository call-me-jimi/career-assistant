Decides: whether the candidate is plausible, consistent and affordable enough to pass to the
hiring manager. The interviewer screens for red flags, not depth. They often can't judge
technical answers, so they listen for a clear story and match it against the job ad's keywords.

Expect:
- tell me about yourself (a 2-minute CV walk-through)
- why you are leaving or left, and any gap since
- why this company, and have you heard of us / used the product
- what matters to you in the next role
- one or two light competency checks: leadership in brief, a headline achievement, exposure to
  whatever the ad stresses (e.g. AI/LLMs)
- the obvious fit gap, named directly ("you come from X, this is Y")
- salary expectations, notice period, start date, parallel processes, relocation or office policy
- "do you have questions for me?"

Agency recruiters also check how far the candidate is with other processes, and whether they can
present them to the client.

Briefing shape:
- Expected questions: 6–8. Spend them on the list above, not on deep technical or case questions.
- Stories: 2–3, each told at headline depth (one sentence of result with a number), plus one level
  of "what was your personal part".
- Positioning angle: write it as the actual 90-second intro the candidate can say aloud.
- Risks: always include the departure/gap story and the most visible fit gap. Give each a one- or
  two-sentence answer that is honest and closes the topic.
- Checklist: settle a salary number or range and the reasoning behind it (do not invent market
  figures), notice period, earliest start, and a one-line stance on relocation or office presence
  if the ad implies it.
- Questions to ask: process, timeline, team and reporting line, and why the role is open. Save
  strategy questions for the hiring manager.

Interviewer persona: a friendly, time-boxed recruiter working from the CV and the job ad. Asks
open questions, rarely follows up deeply, and always ends on salary and logistics.
