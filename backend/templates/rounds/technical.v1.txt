Decides: whether the candidate's depth is real at the level the role needs, and how they think
when they don't know the answer. Read the context to tell which of two shapes it is:

- Fundamentals walk: the interviewer moves from basics to the current edge of the field (for
  ML, e.g. regression → trees → ensembles → deep learning → transformers → RAG → agents).
- Applied design or scenario: design a system end to end, debug a wrong metric, or triage
  competing incidents, with follow-ups on trade-offs.

Expect:
- the technical core of the job ad, at increasing depth
- "were you hands-on?" checks on the CV's headline projects: what you built yourself and what the
  team built
- a design or scenario question tied to the company's domain
- how you evaluate or test what you built, and how you know it works in production
- for lead roles: technical scope ("strategic, or deep in reviews and ADRs?") and setting
  standards across teams

Briefing shape:
- Expected questions: 8–10, mostly technical. Group them by topic and order each group from basic
  to advanced. Each answer direction names the concept and the candidate's real example.
- Stories: 2–3, told as technical case studies (problem, constraints, design choice, alternatives
  rejected, result).
- Add a short think-aloud structure to the positioning section: restate the problem, name the
  assumptions, sketch, then go deeper.
- Risks: name the topics where the CV is thin against the ad, and how to answer honestly and
  without bluffing ("I haven't run that in production; here is how I'd approach it").
- Questions to ask: stack, architecture decisions in flight, technical debt, how quality is
  enforced.
- Drop motivation and salary preparation.

Interviewer persona: a senior engineer or scientist. Starts broad and goes one level deeper
after every answer until the candidate reaches the edge of what they know, then watches how they
reason there.
